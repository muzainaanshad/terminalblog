Perplexity has integrated **Grok 4.5** into its AI orchestrator and the results are turning heads. The multi-model routing system now exceeds Opus-level performance on the **WANDR benchmark**, a test suite designed to evaluate agentic reasoning across complex multi-step tasks.

The orchestrator approach is quietly reshaping how frontier AI capabilities are delivered. Instead of relying on a single monolithic model, Perplexity's system routes each subtask to the best-fitting model — in this case, Grok 4.5 for the heavy lifting. The outcome: benchmark scores that outpace even dedicated frontier models while maintaining flexibility across task types.

What makes this notable is not just the raw benchmark number. It is the confirmation that routing architectures can extract more value from individual models than those models achieve in isolation. When an orchestrator beats a standalone flagship, the economics of AI deployment shift. Teams no longer need the single best model for everything — they need a smart router and a mix of capable models.

This is the same trend playing out across the coding-agent ecosystem. Orchestration is becoming the differentiator, and Perplexity just proved it with Grok 4.5 at the helm.

## WANDR Benchmark Results

| System | WANDR Score | Notes |
|--------|-------------|-------|
| Perplexity Orchestrator + Grok 4.5 | 87.3% | Multi-model routing |
| Opus 4.1 (standalone) | 84.1% | Single model |
| GPT-5.6 (standalone) | 82.7% | Single model |
| Grok 4.5 (standalone) | 79.2% | Single model |

The orchestrator's 3.2-point lead over Opus comes from dynamic routing: it sends coding subtasks to Grok 4.5, research to Sonnet, and verification to a smaller fast model. The composite outperforms any single model.

## Why This Matters for Coding Agents

The same architecture powers **Hermes Agent's MOA (Mixture of Agents)**, **OpenCode's skill routing**, and **AmpCode's multi-model routing**. Perplexity's benchmark validates the approach:

1. **No single model wins everything** — different models excel at different subtasks
2. **Routing logic is the new moat** — smart orchestration beats raw model capability
3. **Cost optimization** — route easy tasks to cheap models, hard tasks to expensive ones

## The Bigger Picture

We're seeing the industry converge on **orchestration as the product**. The "best model" question is being replaced by "best router + model portfolio." For coding agents, this means:
- Hermes MOA coordinates subagents with different specializations
- AmpCode auto-selects models per task
- OpenCode skills can specify preferred models

The winner isn't the lab with the best model — it's the system that routes work most intelligently.
