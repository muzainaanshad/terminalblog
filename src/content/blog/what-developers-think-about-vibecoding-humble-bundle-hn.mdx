## What Happened: Humble Bundle's Security Meltdown

On November 12, 2026, a thread hit Hacker News titled *"Humble Bundle just killed most of their security through vibecoding."* The post linked to a SpaceBattles forum writeup detailing how Humble Bundle's recent site rewrite — reportedly built with AI assistance — had stripped away fundamental security features:

- **No 2FA** on accounts
- **Password changes without confirmation** — no previous password required, no double-entry of new password
- **One-click payments enabled** (skipping checkout confirmation, which violates EU requirements)
- **Broken login flows** — users couldn't authenticate via Google or email
- **Account deletion impossible** — the feature simply didn't work
- **Order history gone** — purchase records vanished

The thread exploded to 173 comments in hours. But the interesting part isn't the security failures — it's what developers *said* about them.

---

## The Core Debate: Tool vs. Human Responsibility

The comment thread fractured along a sharp line. One camp argued: **"AI doesn't produce bad software, people do."** The other: **"AI makes producing bad software easier."**

### The "It's the Human" Camp

> *"AI doesn't produce bad software, people do. It's up to the people involved in the process, to gatekeep AI to not ship bad software."*

This view treats AI like any other tool — a saw, an IDE, a compiler. If you cut your finger, you don't blame the saw. Several commenters pushed this hard:

> *"Does your IDE of choice produce bad software?"* — challenging the idea that autocomplete or Copilot bears responsibility for what ships.

> *"Vibe-coding is a skill (like tying shoelaces), not a tool. And there's always room for improvement."*

The argument: developers who skip review, skip testing, and skip understanding what they're shipping are the failure point. The AI is just the accelerator.

### The "AI Changes the Equation" Camp

> *"AI makes producing bad software easier."*

This camp acknowledges human responsibility but argues the *dynamic* shifts. Key points:

**Speed without understanding.** One commenter nailed it: *"GenAI stresses this loop even more, and no human can effectively deal with the level of output you need to fit in to continue effectively shipping. This can only mean more bugs rather than fewer bugs."*

**The greenfield trap.** *"People are rebuilding working things from scratch with AI just because they can. AI eroded the greenfield advantage of rewriting a project from scratch because a rewrite generally meant that the code went through the fingers of the new developer. With AI that advantage is gone."*

**Management pressure.** Multiple comments pointed to the same structural problem: *"Delusional bosses who decide that human work is worthless in comparison to 5 'ultraclaudes' running in a loop for a week is what produces bad software."* The MBA mantra: cost reduction this quarter > potential costs next quarter.

## What "Vibecoding" Actually Means — And Why It's Confusing

The term itself became a punching bag. One commenter proposed retiring it entirely:

> *"The term 'vibe-coding', to some, implies non-seriousness. It implies fickleness. It implies slop."*

Their proposed replacement taxonomy:
- **L Code** — Code generated by an LLM
- **L Coder** — Someone who codes in natural language via instructions to an LLM
- **L Coding** — The act of prompting an LLM to generate code
- **heLl Code** — L code that has never been, and will likely never be, checked by someone who knows the language. *Potentially dangerous.*

The distinction matters. Several developers drew a line between **using AI as a tool** (you know what you're asking for, you review the output, you catch the fail-open permission bug) versus **vibecoding** (you don't look at the code, you only care about the browser result).

> *"I've asked for authentication systems several times and they've tended to come with pretty good feature-sets. Perhaps it's because I know what I'm asking for. A vibe coder can't."*

## The Surprising Consensus: Rewrite Culture Is the Real Villain

Buried in the thread, a different culprit emerged: **the rewrite itself.**

> *"This is something I don't get. The old development process was slow, but it was mostly safe and reliable. People are rebuilding working things from scratch with AI just because they can."*

> *"AI eroded the greenfield advantage of rewriting a project from scratch... you are better off incrementally improving the old code base to minimize overall maintenance costs."*

Multiple commenters noted Humble Bundle had laid off their dev team years ago. The new site wasn't "AI gone wrong" — it was **no senior engineers left to review the AI output**, combined with management demanding a full rewrite instead of iterative improvement.

> *"My large orgs entire marketing strategy is now run by an MBA using Claude to generate 'idea and best practice docs.' The process that lead us to not having a CMO or any other marketing leadership with domain knowledge? Consulting partnerships using MBAs."*

The pattern: expertise removed → AI fills the gap → nobody understands the system → catastrophic failure.

## The Security-Specific Takeaways

Developers with security backgrounds had specific, actionable critiques:

**Authentication is not a vibecoding task.** *"You do not code your authentication system, in 99% of cases. Take an external provider or a selfhosted OSS project."* Multiple comments emphasized: rolling your own auth in 2026 — AI-assisted or not — is malpractice.

**Fail-open is the default failure mode.** One developer shared: *"I just used Claude Opus 5 High to refactor our permissions model to a hierarchical one. In the validation function, if a part of a permission grant was missing in the hierarchy, claude happily granted them approval instead of rejecting, fail open for permissions. Always review the code an LLM creates."*

**Testing ≠ checking the browser.** *"So as I understand it, vibecoding means you don't look at the code it produces, you care only about the end results, e.g. website through a browser. But in this case, they seem to have skipped that as well."*

## What This Means for You

If you're using AI coding agents — Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Copilot — here's what the Humble Bundle thread teaches:

### 1. **Never vibecode auth, payments, or security boundaries**
Use Auth0, Clerk, Stripe, Supabase Auth. The liability surface is too large and the failure modes are invisible until exploited.

### 2. **Review is not optional — it's the job**
The "I'll just check the browser" approach misses:
- Fail-open permission logic
- Missing rate limits
- Exposed internal endpoints
- Token storage flaws
- CSRF gaps

### 3. **Incremental > Rewrite**
AI makes greenfield *feel* easy. It's not. The old codebase encodes years of bug fixes, edge cases, and regulatory compliance. Throwing it away discards institutional knowledge no LLM has.

### 4. **Push back on "AI replaces engineers" mandates**
If leadership wants to fire the team and keep the AI, show them this thread. The Humble Bundle disaster cost them users, trust, and likely legal exposure — all to save on engineering headcount.

### 5. **Define your own "L Coding" standards**
Borrow the taxonomy:
- **L Code** = AI-generated, reviewed, tested, understood
- **heLl Code** = AI-generated, shipped sight-unseen
- **Your policy**: No heLl Code in production. Ever.

## The Uncomfortable Truth

The thread's most upvoted insight wasn't about AI at all:

> *"Most software teams I've seen were pushing things they don't understand well before AI."*

AI didn't create the competence crisis. It exposed it. Teams that understood their systems use AI to go faster. Teams that didn't use AI to go faster *into a wall*.

The Humble Bundle rewrite didn't fail because of "vibecoding." It failed because **nobody who understood the system remained to catch the AI's mistakes.**
