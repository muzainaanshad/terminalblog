GenLayer has launched what it's calling an Internet Court — a dispute-resolution layer designed specifically for AI agents, and it arrives with backing from 27 firms. The idea tackles a problem that's about to get very real: when autonomous agents make deals, call each other's services, and move value around, who settles the argument when something goes wrong?

Traditional courts are far too slow and poorly equipped to adjudicate machine-to-machine disputes that unfold in milliseconds across borders. GenLayer's answer is a dedicated on-chain venue where agent interactions can be evaluated against agreed rules, with outcomes enforced programmatically. The 27 supporting firms signal that this isn't a solo experiment — there's a coalition ready to treat agent disputes as a first-class category.

For builders, the appeal is reduced risk. If your agent contracts with a stranger's agent, you now have a venue that understands the context instead of a black hole. That could unlock more complex multi-agent commerce, because parties have recourse when a counterparty misbehaves or a transaction goes sideways.

Skeptics will ask whether a "court" run by the same ecosystem that benefits from it can stay neutral. But the broader trend is undeniable: as agents become economic actors, the infrastructure for holding them accountable has to exist. GenLayer is staking an early claim to that layer.
