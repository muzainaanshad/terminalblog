Ollama has closed a $65 million raise, and the headline number undersells the real story: the open-source AI toolkit has now passed 9 million builders. That combination — serious funding and a massive grassroots user base — puts Ollama in rare territory, straddling the line between scrappy community project and infrastructure heavyweight.

For the uninitiated, Ollama made running large language models on your own machine almost trivial. One command pulls a model; a second starts a local server. That simplicity turned it into the default on-ramp for developers who wanted privacy, offline capability, or just freedom from per-token billing. Nine million builders suggests the "run it locally" movement is no longer a niche hobby — it's a mainstream default for a huge slice of the developer population.

The fresh capital points to where Ollama is headed next. Running models at home is table stakes; the company is signaling ambition to build the tooling layer that sits around those models — distribution, versioning, and the glue that lets local AI plug into real products. Investors are effectively betting that local-first AI keeps eating share from cloud-only approaches.

The risk is staying true to the open-source ethos that earned the community in the first place. Spend the money well and Ollama becomes the nervous system of local AI. Misstep, and the builders who made it will simply fork and move on.
