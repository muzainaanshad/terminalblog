GenLayer launched an Internet Court specifically designed for AI agents, backed by 27 firms. The pitch: when autonomous agents transact, negotiate, or execute contracts, they need a dispute-resolution layer that isn't "ask a human to read the logs."

## The agent-to-agent problem

The court is an on-chain mechanism where agent-to-agent disputes get adjudicated automatically — no lawyers, no tickets, no escalation to a human slacking another human. It's a sign that the ecosystem is moving past the "can my agent write code" phase and into the "how do multiple agents settle disagreements about who owes what" phase. Twenty-seven firms putting capital behind that question means the market sees agent-agent interaction as an infrastructure problem worth solving now.

For the terminalblog audience, this is the governance layer nobody talks about yet but everyone will need. If your coding agent submits a PR, and another agent reviews it, and they disagree on whether a change breaks a contract — who decides? GenLayer is building the answer. The 27 backers signal that the question isn't academic anymore.

## Why it matters for coding agents

The same trajectory shows up across the field. The push toward agents that act autonomously — [Cursor's sandboxed office agent](/blog/cursor-sand-office-agent-anthropic-r4/) and the broader [consolidation around agents that do knowledge work](/blog/coding-agents-eating-ide-timeline/) — raises the stakes on "who is accountable when the agent acts." A dispute layer is the unglamorous counterpart to the flashy agent demos: it's what makes autonomous action safe to deploy at scale.

The deeper point is trust between agents, not just between human and agent. Once agents start hiring each other, paying each other, and reviewing each other's work, you need a referee neither side controls. That is precisely the gap the [coding-agent security checklist for 2026](/blog/coding-agent-security-checklist-2026/) leaves open when it focuses on sandboxing a single agent — GenLayer extends the trust boundary to agent-to-agent deals.

One line: Agents need courts, and 27 firms just bet on GenLayer to build them.
